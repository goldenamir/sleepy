# Market research brief

## Commission and boundaries

This is a specification for future research under phase 1 of [[Business development plan]]. It contains no market findings. The user explicitly requested planning rather than research execution.

The founder must select the initial geography, research budget, timebox, and customer hypotheses before execution. Sweden is a candidate, not an approved market. Compare international alternatives where relevant, while keeping local buying conditions distinct.

## Decisions the research must support

1. Which customer group experiences a specific recurring problem?
2. How important is it compared with other priorities, and what do people do now?
3. Which existing alternatives already solve it well?
4. Is there a sufficiently useful gap that could support a focused offer?
5. Who uses, chooses, and pays for the solution?
6. Can we reach those buyers at an affordable cost?
7. Which data and trust requirements affect adoption?
8. Is the reachable opportunity large enough for the founder's chosen business ambition?

## Research work packages

| Package | Future activities | Required output | Limitation to retain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer/problem | Interviews about recent behavior, workarounds, frequency, cost, and switching | Segment-specific problem evidence and contrary cases | Convenience samples are not representative prevalence estimates |
| Existing alternatives | Dated desk research and permitted hands-on comparison of the same task | Alternative matrix with strengths, gaps, price, region, setup effort, and evidence | Vendor claims and observed behavior must be distinguished |
| Market sizing | Find relevant local population/device-use/customer data; define eligibility and access constraints | Bottom-up range with cited denominators, assumptions, and sensitivity | People with sleep apnea are not automatically paying customers |
| Buyer and pricing | Investigate current spending, budget ownership, procurement, and later response to a concrete offer | Payer map and a proposed price/offer experiment | Hypothetical willingness to pay is weaker than purchasing behavior |
| Distribution | Assess where the selected audience can legitimately be reached, channel effort, and conversion steps | Ranked channels with costs/assumptions and a small test design | Community membership or views are not acquired customers |
| Trust and delivery | Ask about data access, privacy expectations, useful explanations, and acceptable setup burden | Adoption constraints linked to [[Data source feasibility]] | Preferences do not resolve technical or legal feasibility |

Initial alternatives to investigate include myAir, OSCAR, Apple Health/Watch, Sleep Cycle, other relevant products discovered during research, and the customer's actual manual or non-digital workaround. Their capabilities and pricing have not been evaluated for this planning document.

## Proposed fieldwork design

Start with 15–20 individual CPAP users, reviewing the method after the first five interviews. Recruit across therapy experience and technology familiarity, including people happy with current tools and people who avoid additional apps. Once a promising segment emerges, concentrate additional interviews there.

Add 3–5 ecosystem conversations only where they answer a specific question. A clinician's opinion about usefulness is not a clinic purchasing commitment. A B2B opportunity needs an identified buyer, decision process, budget, and procurement evidence.

Use [[Customer discovery]] and [[Interview]]. Ask about the last actual occurrence before showing a concept. Record anonymous participant IDs, recruitment source, eligibility, observations, interpretations, counterexamples, and follow-up commitments. Keep a denominator for every count.

Research interviews initially need no raw sleep exports or medical records. Decide what information is necessary, explain the activity to participants, and arrange appropriate handling before collecting sensitive information. Identifiable records belong outside the shared vault.

## Market-sizing method

Define the target segment first. Build a range through separate steps:

- Relevant users in the chosen geography, with source date, population definition, and coverage.
- Fraction for whom the selected problem and compatible setup apply.
- Fraction realistically reachable through identified channels.
- Adoption and payment scenarios supported by progressively stronger evidence.
- Plausible annual net revenue per customer and delivery capacity.

Show unknowns explicitly. Do not multiply an unsupported global disease-prevalence figure by a subscription price and call it the obtainable market. Avoid double-counting user, payer, and clinic populations.

## Evidence and source standards

Prefer original registry/government data, official product documentation, current vendor pricing, firsthand task tests, and observed customer behavior. Record source URL, publication date, date checked, geography, version/setup, exact claim supported, and limitations.

Use public reviews or community discussion as qualitative leads when appropriate; do not treat their frequency as representative demand or copy identifying health stories into a public repository.

## Deliverables and acceptance

Deliver a concise research memo plus:
- Segment comparison and a recommended initial segment.
- Competitor/alternative matrix covering the customer's core task.
- Bottom-up opportunity model with downside/base/upside assumptions.
- Pricing, buyer, and channel hypotheses ranked by evidence.
- Key negative findings, unresolved uncertainties, and proposed next experiment.
- Updated [[Evidence register]], [[Risk and assumption register]], and [[Decision log]].

Acceptance requires traceable sources, clear fact/assumption separation, recruitment/method disclosure, recorded counterexamples, and a specific Continue/Narrow/Rework/Hold/Stop recommendation. The founder makes the gate decision.
